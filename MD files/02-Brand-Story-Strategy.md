# 02 — Brand Story Strategy
## Jordan Story Tours

**Project role:** Brand and product-experience foundation  
**Core idea:** Jordan is not presented as a list of attractions. It unfolds as a story the traveler enters.

---

# 1. Brand Position

## Brand Name
**Jordan Story Tours**

The word **Story** is the strategic differentiator.

The redesigned brand should make the visitor feel:

> I am not buying transportation between tourist attractions. I am choosing the story I want to live in Jordan.

The current website already uses language such as “Your Journey Starts Here,” “Begin your Story,” “Our Story,” and “Start your story in Jordan.” The redesign should turn this existing seed into a coherent brand system.

---

# 2. Core Brand Promise

## Primary
**Your Story Begins Here.**

## Supporting Thought
**Jordan has been telling stories for thousands of years. Now it is time to write yours.**

## Brand Experience CTA
**Choose Your Jordan Story**

## Booking CTA
**Start My Jordan Story**

Do not replace every practical CTA with poetic language. Where clarity matters, use combinations such as:

**Check Availability**  
*Start your Jordan Story*

---

# 3. Brand Pillars

## Story
Every journey has narrative progression: arrival, discovery, wonder, memory.

## Place
Jordan's landscapes and heritage are the protagonists: Petra, Wadi Rum, Dead Sea, Amman, Jerash, Madaba, Aqaba and beyond.

## Human Connection
Drivers, guides, local culture, hospitality, food and encounters make the experience personal.

## Trust
Beautiful storytelling must sit on top of precise itineraries, transparent inclusions, verified prices and clear booking conditions.

## Discovery
The interface should reward scrolling and exploration without making users work to find practical information.

---

## The Desert Story
Best for landscape, adventure and Wadi Rum.

Potential experiences:
- Wadi Rum

- Desert camps
- Stargazing
- Petra + Wadi Rum combinations

## The Sacred Story

Potential destinations:

## The Red Sea Story

- Aqaba
- Wadi Rum
- Petra
- Southern itineraries

## The Adventure Story
Potential experiences:
- Wadi Mujib
- Wadi Rum
- Hiking

- Nature-focused itineraries

## The Essential Jordan Story
Short first-time itineraries combining Jordan's most recognizable destinations.

## The Complete Jordan Story
Longer multi-day itineraries connecting north, center and south.

## The Luxury Jordan Story
Premium accommodation, private touring and upgraded experiences.

“Luxury” must correspond to verified service standards, not simply visual branding.

# 5. Homepage Narrative

The homepage should feel like entering Jordan before it becomes a conventional travel interface.

## Prologue — Enter the Story

Full viewport.

Visitor begins inside the Petra Siq.

Minimal interface:
- Logo
- Menu
- Language
- Booking/availability action

Copy:

**JORDAN IS CALLING**

or preferred primary direction:

# Your Story Begins Here.

**Scroll to enter Jordan**

The scene should communicate depth rather than behave like a normal hero slider.

---

# 6. Scroll World

## Chapter 1 — The Siq

The visitor scrolls forward through Petra's narrow canyon.

Visual behavior:
- foreground rock layers move faster than background
- subtle light shifts
- restrained particles/dust
- typography remains minimal
- motion must respond smoothly to scroll
- no autoplay audio

Copy appears sparingly:

**Some stories are read.**  
**Others are lived.**

---

## Chapter 2 — The Treasury Reveal

The Siq opens.

The Treasury appears progressively rather than being visible immediately.

Primary statement:

# A Story Carved in Stone.

Supporting text:

**Petra is only the beginning.**

The visitor continues downward/forward.

---

## Chapter 3 — Petra Becomes Wadi Rum

Rock texture transitions toward open desert.

Potential techniques:
- masked image/video transitions
- parallax layers
- sand/dust transition
- camera-depth illusion
- WebGL only where it creates meaningful depth

Copy:

# Beyond the stone, the desert begins.

---

## Chapter 4 — Wadi Rum

The visual field opens dramatically.

Possible elements:
- distant jeep
- camel silhouettes
- rock formations
- shifting sun
- subtle sand
- day-to-sunset transition

Copy:

# Beneath a Million Stars.

**The Desert Story**

CTA:
**Explore Wadi Rum Stories**

---

## Chapter 5 — Dead Sea

Desert tones transition into water/reflection.

Motion becomes slower and calmer.

Copy:

# Float Between Earth and Sky.

**The Dead Sea Story**

Avoid making unsupported superlative or health claims.

---

## Chapter 6 — Jordan Revealed

The cinematic sequence resolves into a stylized interactive map of Jordan.

The visitor now understands that the visual prologue was navigation, not decoration.

Destinations can illuminate:
- Amman
- Jerash
- Madaba
- Mount Nebo
- Dead Sea
- Petra
- Wadi Rum
- Aqaba

Final transition:

# Now Choose Your Jordan Story.

This leads into the commercial homepage.

# 7. Performance Rule for the Scroll World

The cinematic experience must **never destroy Core Web Vitals**.

Use progressive enhancement:

### High-capability desktop
Full scroll choreography, layered depth and carefully limited WebGL where justified.

### Standard mobile
Reduced layers, optimized short media sequences, CSS/GPU transforms.

### Low-power / reduced-motion
Static or gently transitioning hero images with the same narrative and content.

Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.

Do not:
- load one huge uncompressed hero movie
- block page interaction while animation initializes
- hide SEO text inside canvas
- require WebGL for navigation
- use animation that causes layout shift
- autoplay sound

# 9. Product Naming System

Do not replace keyword-rich tour names with only poetic names.

Use two layers.

## SEO / Functional Name
**Petra and Wadi Rum Tour from Amman**

## Story Name
**Two Icons, One Story**

Display example:

# Petra & Wadi Rum Tour from Amman
### Two Icons. One Unforgettable Jordan Story.

This protects search intent while strengthening brand memory.

Another example:

# 5-Day Jordan Tour: Petra, Dead Sea & Jerash
### The Classic Jordan Story

---

# 10. Itinerary as Chapters

A tour program should feel like a narrative without hiding operational information.

Instead of only:

**Day 3 — Petra**

Use:

### Day 3
# A Story Carved in Stone
**Petra**

Then clearly show:
- departure
- driving
- attractions
- meals
- hotel
- included activities
- optional activities

“Chapter” language may be used visually, but “Day 3” remains visible for clarity and SEO.

Fields:

- Name
- Slug
- Short tagline
- Long description
- Hero media
- Icon
- Sort order
- Active/inactive
- Associated destinations
- Associated tours
- English content
- German content

- OG image

Story Collections are marketing taxonomy; operational tour categories remain separate.

# 13. Traveler Stories

Reviews should become a branded content component:

# Traveler Stories

Use only authentic reviews.

Fields:
- reviewer name/display name
- country where permitted
- traveler type
- tour
- rating
- review
- date
- source
- verification status
- permission/media

Never generate fake reviews.

# 14. Build Your Own Story

User chooses:
- travel dates
- number of days
- travelers
- interests

- budget range

This should coexist with ready-made tours.

# 16. Voice

Jordan Story should sound:

- evocative
- knowledgeable
- warm
- confident
- precise
- locally grounded
- premium without being pretentious

Avoid:
- generic “amazing unforgettable breathtaking” repetition
- keyword stuffing
- exaggerated historical claims
- fake urgency
- excessive exclamation marks
- AI-sounding filler
- calling every package “once in a lifetime”

---

# 17. SEO + Story Rule

Every important page must answer two questions:

## Search Engine
**What exactly is this page about?**

## Traveler
**Why should I care about this experience?**

Example:

SEO says:
**Private Petra Tour from Amman**

Brand says:
**Walk Through a Story Carved in Stone.**

We need both.

---

# 18. AEO Rule

Important pages should answer real travel questions directly before expanding.

Example:

### Can you visit Petra and Wadi Rum in one day from Amman?

Provide a concise factual answer based on the actual tour, then explain timing, driving and experience details.

Answer blocks should be:
- clear
- factual
- self-contained
- useful
- consistent with protected business data

---

# 19. GEO Rule

Create explicit relationships between:

**Jordan Story Tours**
→ offers a **tour**
→ departing from **Amman**
→ visiting **Petra**
→ continuing to **Wadi Rum**
→ with verified **duration / inclusions / price basis**

The website should be easy for humans, Google and generative systems to interpret.

Use consistent entities, structured content, author/about/trust signals, factual destination hubs and appropriate schema.

---

# 20. Multilingual Brand

The Story idea must work naturally in every language; slogans should be localized, not mechanically translated.

Recommended initial architecture:
- English
- German

German SEO titles should target German search behavior while the emotional Story layer is professionally localized.

Admin must track:
- published
- draft
- translation needs review
- source updated after translation

---

# 21. Visual Direction

Think:

**cinematic Jordan + editorial travel journal + premium interactive storytelling**

Not:
- generic booking template
- excessive glassmorphism
- animation everywhere
- gold-on-black luxury cliché
- crowded card grids

Materials/textures can take inspiration from:
- Petra sandstone
- desert earth
- parchment/map detail
- Dead Sea mineral textures
- night sky
- Jordanian craft patterns

Use these subtly.

---

# 22. Motion Language

Motion should represent travel:

- path drawing → route
- forward depth → entering a place
- reveal → discovery
- sand → desert transition
- ripple → water/Dead Sea
- stars → Wadi Rum night
- map trace → itinerary
- chapter transition → tour day progression

Animations must have purpose.

# 24. Non-Negotiable Brand Rules

1. “Story” is a system, not a word to repeat everywhere.
2. SEO tour names remain explicit.
3. Operational facts always override poetic copy.
4. Animation must improve immersion or understanding.
5. Mobile users receive an equally premium but lighter experience.
6. Every Story must lead toward a useful next action.
7. Accessibility and reduced-motion support are mandatory.
8. Authentic Jordan photography/video is preferred.
9. AI must never invent itinerary facts, reviews, historical claims or inclusions.
10. The final feeling should be: **I can already imagine myself there.**

---

# 25. North Star

The redesign succeeds when a visitor can:

1. **Feel Jordan** through the opening experience.
2. **Understand Jordan** through destination and answer content.
3. **Choose their Story** through intuitive discovery.
4. **Trust the company** through precise information.
5. **Book easily** without the brand experience getting in the way.
